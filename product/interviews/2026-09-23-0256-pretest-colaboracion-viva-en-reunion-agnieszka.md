**🎤 (W1):** To start, tell me about your team: who are they and where are they?

**👤 Agnieszka:** Eleven people. Six here in Wrocław, three in Porto, two in Recife. QA engineers, a couple of automation people.

**🎤 (probe):** What's a typical week of meetings like for you? How many do you lead?

**👤 Agnieszka:** Maybe six I run myself. The weekly test strategy review, go/no-go for each release, bug triage with product. The rest I just attend.

**🎤 (probe, yes/no):** Do people from another company, like suppliers, clients or contractors, sometimes join those meetings?

**👤 Agnieszka:** Yes. Clients, for acceptance sessions.

---

**🎤 (Q1, contradictor version: in the survey she answered Q3 = Never):** The last time your team had to design or decide something together, how did you do it? Where did it happen?

**👤 Agnieszka:** Test strategy for the checkout release, last Thursday. People dropped comments on the Confluence page during the week, then we closed it in the meeting. On Whiteboard, sticky notes, one column per risk area, everybody voting. Took longer than I wanted. Twelve people adding notes at once, it gets laggy, you wait for things to appear. We finished though.

**🎤 (probe):** Who was writing, who was watching?

**👤 Agnieszka:** Everybody writes. That's the rule, otherwise the Porto people go quiet.

**🎤 (probe):** What happened with what you decided afterwards?

**👤 Agnieszka:** I copied it into Azure DevOps and the audit log. Twenty minutes, maybe. Annoying but fine.

**🎤 (native probe, "always"):** Have you ever tried to do something like that inside Teams? What happened that time?

**👤 Agnieszka:** ...I just told you. It was inside Teams.

---

**🎤 (Q2):** Think about the last time the group wanted to work on something together, live, during a meeting, and couldn't do it where or how they wanted. What happened?

**👤 Agnieszka:** Where we wanted... we always do it in Teams, that's not a choice. How we wanted, hm. Two weeks ago, acceptance session with a client. They run everything in their FigJam. From our network the proxy blocked it, so I couldn't get in. We lost twenty minutes, then their PM just shared his screen and I was dictating. I had to write up all the decisions afterwards from memory.

**🎤 (probe):** How often does something like that happen?

**👤 Agnieszka:** With that client, every other session.

---

**🎤 (Q3, branch (b): the Q1 episode was inside Teams):** Think about the last time you used something outside Teams to work together in a meeting. How did you end up with that tool that day?

**👤 Agnieszka:** The client's FigJam. Like I said. Their board, their rules.

**🎤 (probe):** Did anyone suggest another option?

**👤 Agnieszka:** It's their meeting. No.

**🎤 (Whiteboard branch):** What happened the last time you used Whiteboard? Did you use it again?

**👤 Agnieszka:** Did we use it again? We use it every week. I don't understand the question.

---

**🎤 (Q4, following the E3 note: the external-guest episode already came up in Q2, and someone else controlled access):** Was there ever a time it didn't work? What happened that time?

**👤 Agnieszka:** That's the proxy one. Other times I got in, but only with view rights, because their PM forgot to switch me to edit. We noticed ten minutes in.

**🎤 (probe):** What did you do the next time?

**👤 Agnieszka:** I email their PM the day before now. "Please give me edit." Sometimes works.

---

**🎤 (W2):** Is there anything about how you work together in meetings that I didn't ask and should have?

**👤 Agnieszka:** You didn't ask why we don't use Miro. Everybody asks that. Security blocked it two years ago after a bank client's audit. If it were up to the engineers, we'd be on Miro tomorrow.

**🎤 (W3):** Do you know someone else who runs similar meetings and works differently from you?

**👤 Agnieszka:** The platform team lead in Porto. Before the ban they were the heaviest Miro users. They complain the most.
